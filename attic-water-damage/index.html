Attic Water Damage in Mount Vernon

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. For Mount Vernon homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Water droplets or rust on the tips of nails that poke through the roof sheathing
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Our Process

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Trace

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Insulation that is soaked or has mold gets bagged and carried out, giving the wood and the drywall below a chance to dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Insulation That Stops Working

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.
